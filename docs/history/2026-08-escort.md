# The dead who walk with him — an escort without unit orders (R2d)

Prompt **R2d**, against the whole of `ESCORT_SPEC.md` (body plus the binding 2026-08-29 amendment),
`ROGUELITE_REWORK.md` §5, and the header comments in `RallyPoint.gd` and `UndeadCommand.gd` — which,
as the spec says, already argue most of this. R2c was done: the party can bank what it carries.

The spec's own claim is that the escort is **one enum member and one field**, and it is worth
recording that this turned out to be true. The whole feature is:

```gdscript
enum Order { DEFEND, PATROL, ATTACK, ESCORT }
var follow: Object = null

func _process(_delta):
    if follow != null and is_instance_valid(follow):
        position = follow.position
```

Everything downstream — `_advance_bound()`, the arrive epsilon, `_hostile_for()`'s
measure-from-the-point rule, the drawn radius ring — works unchanged, because none of it ever
assumed the point was still. There is no second follow-the-villain system, which is the thing §1.2
exists to prevent.

---

## 1. Why this does not break the pillar

`RallyPoint.gd`'s header made the argument years before this prompt: the player's lever is *a spell
with a target*, not a selection box with a move order, and the dead have no will to override — which
is the entire difference between a skeleton and a recruit. The escort is that same spell anchored to
a man instead of to the ground.

So the scope calls come along unchanged and are asserted rather than assumed:

- **All bound undead, never a chosen subset.** Picking which three skeletons to take is a selection
  UI, and a selection UI is the per-unit control the pillar rules out. `_bind_all()` is untouched.
- **Living recruits never escort in R2.** They have wills. The harness puts a living recruit on the
  roster and asserts the spell does not touch it and it stays in `laborers()`.
- **Nothing is ever added to the escort by hand.** `Necromancer.escort` is a *cache*, rewritten
  every frame from "undead ∧ rallied ∧ the point follows him". Its comment says so, and the harness
  has no test anywhere that appends to it.

That last one is what makes §3's most interesting row true for free: **a skeleton raised at a grave
joins the escort within one frame with no extra code**, because the standing order re-binds every
frame. `LOOT_SITES_SPEC.md` §4's "dormant until escort lands, then retroactively live" is this
landing, and R2a's `RaisedDead` bodies have been standing at gravesides waiting for it.

## 2. The one real refactor

`_hostile_for()` iterated `combat_system.wolves`, so a bound skeleton standing in front of a site
guardian could not see it — which would have made the den fight the escort exists for unwinnable
*by the escort*. `CombatSystem.hostiles()` is now public and unions wolves with live site guardians,
and both the villain's own engage loop (from R2b) and the escort read through it.

The list also already drops a departing wolf, so "let a beaten or fed one go" stopped being a check
restated in `UndeadCommand` and became a property of the hostile list. Nothing downstream hard-codes
a creature type any more, which is the point.

### A second gap found while in there

`_advance_bound()` called `Roaming.step()` **without a world**. Every other mover in the project
passes one — the wolf, the deer, the villain, the guardians — so a bound skeleton was the only thing
on the map that walked through cliffs while the wolf chasing it went around. `UndeadCommand` now
takes a `world` field and passes it through, in all three movement paths (follow, chase, patrol
beat). The harness walks the villain into a wall and asserts the point still tracks him and he did
not end up inside it.

## 3. Stances (amendment 2026-08-29)

**A policy on the spell, never an order to a unit** — which is what keeps it on the right side of
the pillar even though it changes what the dead do. One whole-escort value on the rally point, no
per-skeleton setting, no selection UI:

| Stance | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Defensive** (default) | engage only once the party — him or any escort — has been struck |
| **Aggressive** | engage anything hostile inside the ring, the body's original behaviour |

Without it a party can never walk *past* a den or a guardian without starting the fight, which
becomes fatal once R3 puts patrols and adventurers on the map. Defensive is the default because it
makes the party consistent with the villain's own engage model: nothing the player owns starts a
fight unless they chose it.

**Defensive needed a latch**, and it is worth recording why. "Engage only after the party is struck"
is a statement about a past event, so without something to clear it the escort would stay aggressive
for the rest of the run after one scratch. `_party_struck` is set from `damage_shown` — the signal
the policy layer already fires for the floating numbers, so no second announcement was needed — and
cleared when nothing hostile is left inside the ring. Switching *to* Defensive also clears it: the
player just said "stop starting things", and holding the latch would ignore them for as long as the
fight lasted.

The gate lives in `_hostile_for()` rather than in `_advance_bound()`, so every consumer of "what may
this unit attack" inherits it — including anything added later.

## 4. Cover the retreat

The only genuinely new behaviour, and the one thing the dead do that the player did not ask for.
Below `Combat.FLEE_HP_FRACTION` the escort **interposes**: each member targets a point between him
and the nearest hostile (60% of the way toward the threat — standing exactly on the midpoint leaves
a gap either side at any real spacing) rather than the hostile itself, and they do not break off.

Undead don't rout. The living-recruit flee rule explicitly does not apply to them, so "cover" is
positioning, not morale. It ends when he heals past the threshold or the hostiles are gone.

**It overrides stance in both directions** — instinct is not policy — which the harness asserts by
running the whole sequence twice, once per stance. And it is announced: *"The dead close ranks."*,
once on each transition, because §7 says the one behaviour the player did not ask for must never be
silent.

## 5. Legibility (§7)

- **The rally marker follows him**, drawing its radius ring as it always has, in a fourth colour
  distinct from the other three. The leash is visible at all times, so "why didn't they chase it"
  keeps the visible answer it already had.
- **The marker's panel** gains an anchored-to-him row and the current stance with its blurb, but
  only while it is actually escorting.
- **The villain's panel** gains the Escort cast (and its re-anchor), plus the two stance buttons
  with the current one greyed.
- **Toggling writes a log line**, as does the cover transition and an escort death.

Escort is deliberately **not** in `rally_actions`' order list: a point already anchored to a man has
no ground to be re-ordered on, and the cast belongs on his own panel. It also needs no placement
mode and no new branch in Main's input arbitration — because the target is the man casting it, which
is the whole reason it is one button.

## 6. Death

No bespoke path: an escort member dies through `Combat.exchange()` exactly as any worker does.
`escort_member_lost` is the escort-shaped announcement on top of the existing `worker_destroyed`, so
the log can say the loss was a long way from home and took a load with it.

One real gap surfaced here: `abandon_trip()` releases the node claim but **deliberately does not
zero the carried load** — `_bind_all()` has always zeroed it separately for exactly that reason — so
a dead escort was discarded still holding your gold. Functionally the load was gone (the unit leaves
the roster and the cache), but "it is gone" was only true of the roster, not of the object.
`_resolve_defeat()` now clears it, which makes the assertion honest and stops anything holding a
stale reference from finding phantom cargo.

## 7. Verification

`tools/verify_escort.tscn` — **54 assertions**, run as a scene, covering §10's list:

| Claim | How |
|---|---|
| one enum member, one field | the order, the 2.5-cell radius sitting between DEFEND and PATROL, name/blurb/colour/radius all answering through the same accessors |
| binds undead and only undead | a living recruit on the roster stays unrallied and in the labour pool |
| the labour cost | every bound skeleton fails `can_labor()`, and gets it back on dismiss |
| the leash | the point tracks him within one frame across 60 steps **and through a blocking-terrain slide**; the escort catches up and keeps station |
| a raised corpse joins | a worker added to the roster is bound and in the escort one frame later, with no explicit add |
| the refactor | the den's guardian is a `SiteGuardian` and not a `Wolf`, `hostiles()` includes it, and a bound skeleton can target it |
| stances | Defensive ignores a guardian inside the ring until the party is struck, then engages; Aggressive engages on entry; the stance is one value on the spell and not a field on any unit |
| cover the retreat | fires below the threshold in **both** stances, announces once, moves them toward the threat, does not break off, and stands down on healing |
| death | announced as an escort loss, leaves the roster and the cache, load zeroed |

Full suite green: `verify_escort` 54, `verify_sortie` 66, `verify_villain_combat` 65,
`verify_loot_tables` 515, `verify_terrain` 278, `check_fog_and_minimap` 50, `smoke_site_actions` 26,
`check_sprite_scales` 122, `verify_stats` 505, `verify_combat_feedback` 31. Headless boot clean, all
six travel rows in band.

### A flake worth recording

The harness passed seven runs in a row, then failed three assertions once — in a batch where several
harnesses ran back to back. The cause: **the live scene keeps running across every
`await get_tree().process_frame`**, and site guardians prowl. Positioning the villain relative to a
guardian and *then* awaiting means the distance between them is whatever the prowl did in the
meantime, and under load that is further.

The fix is to pin the guardian at its post and place the villain relative to *that* immediately
before asserting. The rules under test are about targeting and stance, not about where a wolf
happened to wander. Five consecutive clean runs since, plus a re-run of the batch that caught it.

**The general lesson for this repo's harnesses:** anything positioned relative to a live, moving
object must be re-pinned after the last `await`, or it is measuring the scheduler.

## 8. Not built

`ESCORT_SPEC.md` §9's line about flipping `LAIR_AURA_PROTECTS_VILLAIN` is **satisfied by R2b, not by
new code here** — the aura became a position test and the flag was deleted in that pass. Escort
behaviour reads the same test if it ever needs to, and this pass reintroduced nothing.

Also untouched, per the spec: no corpse recovery (a spell, so R5), no `MAX_ESCORT` cap (the roster
*is* the party size, and a cap would hide the cost the spell exists to expose), and living
volunteers stay a reputation-era feature for after R3's axes.

## 9. Needs a human

§10's two questions, and neither is answerable headless:

1. **Does the party read as a party at world zoom?** `ESCORT_RADIUS_PX` at 2.5 cells is the single
   number that decides it — tight enough to look like a group, loose enough not to conga-line
   through a mountain pass.
2. **Does the leash feel like protection or like a tether?** The skeleton walks 0.9 cells/sec
   against his 1.0, so outrunning your own guard is a real decision on the return leg. If it proves
   merely annoying rather than tense, the tunable is closing that gap — *not* removing the leash.

Worth watching alongside them: whether Defensive is the right default. It is the safe answer and it
makes the party consistent with his own engage model, but a player who expects their skeletons to
deal with an approaching wolf may read the first Defensive stand-off as the escort being broken.
The stance is on the panel and toggling logs, which is the mitigation; if it still reads wrong, the
amendment already names flipping the default as the tunable.
